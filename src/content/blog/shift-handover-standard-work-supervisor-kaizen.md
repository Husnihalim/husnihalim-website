<p>In most Malaysian plants, shift handover happens — but repeat problems still happen too. The reason is simple: the handover is often a recap (“today got problem”), not a control plan (“this condition must be protected in the first two hours”).</p>
<p>When the next shift does not inherit the <em>condition</em>, they start blind. Then the same downtime, same quality hold, same material shortage, and the same late recovery repeats.</p>

<h2>Why Shift Handover Fails (Even with Good People)</h2>
<p>Shift handover fails when it is treated as communication instead of control. Communication tells a story. Control protects output by preventing repeat losses.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Abnormalities are mentioned but not owned.</strong> No owner + due time = the issue survives into the next shift.</li>
  <li><strong>Loss reasons are too generic.</strong> “Machine problem” cannot be repeated and tracked; “jam at station 3 after changeover” can.</li>
  <li><strong>No first-two-hours plan.</strong> The next shift starts “as usual” and loses the recovery window.</li>
  <li><strong>No confirmation at the actual place.</strong> A board update is not the same as seeing the abnormal point at the line.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The Supervisor Standard Work: A 10–15 Minute Handover</h2>
<p>Good handover does not need a long meeting. It needs a standard. Use the same structure every day so the team learns what “complete” looks like.</p>

<h3>Minimum Viable Handover (What to Cover)</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Handover item</th><th>What “good” looks like</th><th>Common failure</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Actual vs plan</td><td>Last 2 hours vs hourly plan + clear gap</td><td>Only end-of-shift total, no timing</td></tr>
<tr><td>Top 1–2 losses</td><td>Repeat reason + where it happens</td><td>Generic “machine / quality / manpower”</td></tr>
<tr><td>Open abnormalities</td><td>List + owner + due time</td><td>“Pending” with no owner</td></tr>
<tr><td>Quality status</td><td>Hold points, rework, approval needed</td><td>Next shift discovers later at packing</td></tr>
<tr><td>Material constraints</td><td>What will run out, when, and escalation done</td><td>“Material late” without timing</td></tr>
<tr><td>Maintenance actions</td><td>Work in progress + temporary containment</td><td>Next shift repeats calls for same stop</td></tr>
<tr><td>Manpower placement</td><td>Who is where + skill gaps + coverage plan</td><td>Assume same manpower then scramble</td></tr>
<tr><td>First 2 hours plan</td><td>What must be checked by what time</td><td>Start “as usual” and get surprised</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<h2>The “First Two Hours” Checklist (Stops Repeat Firefighting)</h2>
<p>If you can only tighten one output, tighten this: the next shift must start with a clear first-two-hours plan. That is where repeat losses are prevented.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Hour 0–1:</strong> Confirm the top abnormal point at the line (not on the board).</li>
  <li><strong>Hour 0–1:</strong> Confirm critical material availability for the first 2 hours.</li>
  <li><strong>Hour 0–1:</strong> Confirm quality hold/approval status and who can release it.</li>
  <li><strong>Hour 1–2:</strong> If downtime repeats, capture the exact reason and location (so it can be solved, not repeated).</li>
  <li><strong>Hour 1–2:</strong> Confirm hourly output vs plan and escalate early if the gap is growing.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to Coach Operators Without Blame During Handover</h2>
<p>Handover is a high-risk moment for blame: the shift is tired and the next shift wants an explanation. Keep the standard: discuss the process condition, not the person.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>What changed?</strong> When did the condition shift?</li>
  <li><strong>Where is it happening?</strong> Which station or point of control?</li>
  <li><strong>What is the temporary containment?</strong> How do we protect the next two hours?</li>
  <li><strong>What small experiment will we test?</strong> One countermeasure to try and learn from.</li>
</ul>
